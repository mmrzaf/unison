# Maintenance classification and control

Every accepted maintenance objective uses one Work Order and exactly one primary maintenance class: preventive, corrective, inspection, or improvement. Trigger, origin, urgency, priority, discipline, execution risk, deferral consequence, and planning route remain separate dimensions.

## Keep one Work Order model and express differences through classification and conditional rules

Waiotech must keep one Work Order model and express differences through classification and conditional rules.

Waiotech has one canonical Work Order model for all accepted maintenance work.

Preventive, corrective, inspection, and improvement work may require different planning, evidence, approval, and completion rules, but they remain classifications of one Work Order rather than separate Work Order entities.

## Use Work Order as the only representation of accepted maintenance work

Waiotech must use Work Order as the only representation of accepted maintenance work.

A Work Order represents maintenance work that Waiotech has accepted, authorized, or generated for execution.

It is the authoritative unit for:

- planning;
- responsibility;
- scheduling;
- readiness;
- execution;
- evidence;
- verification;
- costing;
- closeout.

There must be no accepted maintenance need outside a Work Order.

## Keep the four maintenance classes as a closed product catalogue

Waiotech must keep the four maintenance classes as a closed product catalogue.

Waiotech uses exactly four canonical maintenance classes:

- preventive;
- corrective;
- inspection;
- improvement.

The classes are defined by the intent of the work, not by the physical activity being performed.

## Preventive maintenance

Waiotech must classify work as preventive when its primary intent is failure prevention or preservation of required function.

Preventive maintenance preserves required function or reduces failure likelihood before a known failure or unacceptable condition requires restoration.

Preventive work may be time-based, usage-based, condition-informed, or generated by another governed maintenance rule.

Examples include planned lubrication, scheduled replacement, calibration, cleaning, and periodic servicing when their purpose is preventing deterioration or failure.

## Corrective maintenance

Waiotech must classify work as corrective when its primary intent is restoring or correcting an existing unacceptable condition.

Corrective maintenance restores required function or corrects a known failure, defect, or unacceptable condition.

Corrective work may be planned or unplanned. It may originate from a Failure Event, Finding, Work Request, inspection result, or direct authorized creation.

## Inspection maintenance

Waiotech must classify work as inspection when its primary intent is determining or verifying condition.

Inspection maintenance determines, measures, or verifies Asset condition, performance, or compliance without an already accepted corrective objective.

Inspection work may include:

- visual checks;
- measurements;
- tests;
- condition assessments;
- compliance checks;
- structured inspection rounds.

An inspection may discover Findings or Failure Events, but the inspection itself remains complete when the required examination and evidence have been properly recorded.

## Improvement maintenance

Waiotech must classify work as improvement when its primary intent is an intentional enhancement rather than restoration.

Improvement maintenance changes design, capability, maintainability, reliability, safety, or performance beyond restoring the previously required condition.

An improvement may be triggered by repeated failures, operational experience, safety concerns, obsolescence, or performance opportunities.

## Require exactly one maintenance class per Work Order

Waiotech must require exactly one maintenance class per Work Order.

Every Work Order has exactly one primary maintenance class.

Tasks and Procedures inside the Work Order may include different activity types, but they do not create additional maintenance classes.

When one proposed job contains independently managed objectives with different maintenance intent, it should be split into linked Work Orders.

## Represent activity types separately from maintenance class

Waiotech must represent activity types separately from maintenance class.

They describe the activity, method, Procedure, or job type rather than the maintenance intent.

For example:

- scheduled lubrication may be preventive;
- leakage testing may be inspection;
- calibration after an identified error may be corrective;
- equipment redesign during overhaul may be improvement.

## Allow ordinary class changes before execution and governed correction afterward

Waiotech must allow ordinary class changes while work is still being clarified or planned and require governed correction after execution begins.

Before first execution begins, an authorized Actor may correct the maintenance class.

After execution begins, changing the class requires an explicit correction with a reason because the original classification may already have affected planning, reporting, controls, and evidence.

## Represent predictive information through trigger and origin, not maintenance class

Waiotech must represent predictive information through trigger and origin, not maintenance class.

Predictive describes an analytical trigger or detection method.

The resulting Work Order may be:

- inspection, when further assessment is required;
- preventive, when intervention is performed before unacceptable degradation;
- corrective, when a known unacceptable condition must be restored.

## Represent emergency through urgency and emergency-start rules, not maintenance class

Waiotech must represent emergency through urgency and emergency-start rules, not maintenance class.

Emergency describes required response speed and the use of a governed exception path.

An emergency Work Order still has one of the four maintenance classes, commonly corrective or inspection.

## Trigger

Waiotech must record the primary business trigger independently of maintenance class.

Trigger is the business condition that caused the maintenance need to require attention.

Examples include:

- scheduled interval reached;
- Measurement Point threshold reached;
- defect observed;
- failure occurred;
- predicted degradation;
- compliance obligation;
- improvement proposal.

Trigger explains why the need arose.

## Origin

Waiotech must keep trigger and origin separate, and use Scheduled Work as the origin of Plan-generated Work Orders.

Origin identifies the authoritative source record or creation route through which the Work Order entered Waiotech.

Canonical origins include:

- Scheduled Work;
- Work Request;
- Failure Event;
- Finding;
- direct authorized creation;
- product-defined accepted-work integration contract.

For Plan-generated work, Scheduled Work is the direct Work Order origin. The related Maintenance Plan and Plan Revision remain traceable through that occurrence.

Origin explains where the Work Order came from.

## Require clear trigger and origin before normal release

Waiotech must require clear trigger and origin before normal release.

Both facts must be complete and valid before the Work Order is released for execution.

During early drafting, either may be incomplete while the need is being clarified.

Trigger and origin may have different values. For example:

- maintenance class: corrective;
- trigger: predicted degradation;
- origin: external analytics event.

## Urgency

Waiotech must use routine, urgent, and emergency as the closed urgency catalogue.

Urgency expresses how quickly the need requires response.

The canonical urgency values are:

- **Routine:** may proceed through normal planning and scheduling.
- **Urgent:** requires expedited evaluation or execution within a governed response expectation.
- **Emergency:** requires immediate action to control serious existing or imminent consequence.

Urgency does not determine whether work is ready or authorized.

## Allow governed urgency changes with preserved evidence

Waiotech must allow governed urgency changes with preserved evidence.

Conditions may improve or deteriorate.

Urgency changes must preserve:

- previous urgency;
- new urgency;
- changed by;
- changed at;
- reason.

Lowering urgency requires the same accountability as increasing it because it may delay work.

## Priority

Waiotech must keep priority separate from urgency and allow it to change with recorded reason.

Priority is the relative scheduling order among work that is otherwise eligible to proceed.

Priority may consider:

- urgency;
- deferral consequence;
- Operational Criticality;
- production opportunity;
- dependencies;
- regulatory timing;
- resource availability.

Priority does not override readiness, safety controls, or authorization.

## Work-execution risk

Waiotech must use work-execution risk for controls governing how the work is performed.

Work-execution risk is the evaluated hazard and exposure created by performing the specific work under conditions at evaluation time.

It may control:

- planning depth;
- permits;
- isolations;
- approvals;
- execution controls;
- evidence requirements;
- verification;
- emergency exceptions.

## Deferral consequence

Waiotech must keep deferral consequence separate from work-execution risk.

Deferral consequence is the expected consequence of not completing the work within the required period.

It may include effects on:

- safety;
- environment;
- production;
- quality;
- compliance;
- reliability;
- cost.

Deferral consequence informs urgency, scheduling, escalation, and temporary operating controls.

## Operational Criticality

Waiotech must keep Operational Criticality as an input, not a substitute for job-specific evaluation.

Operational Criticality is the relatively stable potential plant consequence of losing the stable function represented by a Functional Location. When work targets an installed Asset, the effective Operational Criticality comes from that Asset's effective Functional Location context rather than from intrinsic Asset identity.

Criticality may inform:

- planning;
- prioritization;
- maintenance strategy;
- inspection frequency;
- risk evaluation;
- approval requirements.

It does not determine urgency, execution risk, or deferral consequence by itself.

## Derive planned or unplanned status from the release and execution sequence

Waiotech must derive planned or unplanned status from the release and execution sequence.

Planning route is derived from what actually happened, not selected as a label by a planner.

Canonical values:

- **Planned:** the Work Order was normally released before first execution began.
- **Unplanned:** first execution began through an emergency or unreleased exception.

Planning route is recorded when first execution begins and must remain immutable after first execution begins.

A corrective Work Order may be planned, and preventive work may become unplanned if execution begins through an exception.

## Treat emergency as urgency plus a governed exception-start path

Waiotech must treat emergency as urgency plus a governed exception-start path.

Emergency urgency alone does not bypass normal controls.

Emergency execution requires a named emergency-start action performed by an authorized Actor.

The action must record:

- why immediate execution is necessary;
- which normal readiness requirements are incomplete;
- temporary controls;
- responsible Team and coordinator where required;
- retrospective obligations;
- required review and evidence.

Bypassed requirements remain visible and must be resolved according to the emergency workflow.

## Use one primary discipline with optional supporting disciplines, governed outside IAM

Waiotech must use one primary discipline with optional supporting disciplines, governed outside IAM.

Discipline supports technical routing, planning, and responsibility, but it does not define maintenance meaning or IAM authority.

A Work Order has:

- one primary discipline;
- optional supporting disciplines.

Examples include:

- mechanical;
- electrical;
- instrumentation;
- civil;
- operations support.

A discipline label does not grant Permissions.

## Use one product-managed Maintenance discipline catalogue across all Tenants

Waiotech must use one product-managed Maintenance discipline catalogue across all Tenants.

Maintenance discipline is product-fixed.

Waiotech owns one stable discipline catalogue for every Tenant. Tenants may select applicable disciplines but cannot:

- create new discipline codes;
- rename canonical disciplines;
- change their meaning;
- retire them;
- attach custom product behavior to them.

Display labels may be localized without changing the canonical code or meaning.

Adding, changing, or retiring a discipline requires a governed Waiotech product change. A discipline code that has been used remains permanently interpretable in historical records.
